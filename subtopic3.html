🛡️ Stay Safe Online

Learn to spot scams, protect your accounts and keep your money safe.

⚠ Your bank, the police and the government will never ask for your password, PIN or OTP code by phone, SMS or WhatsApp.

Common scams

📦

Fake delivery SMS

"Your parcel is waiting, pay 1 BD here." It asks you to click a link and enter card details.

📞

Fake bank call

Someone pretends to be your bank and asks for the code sent to your phone.

🎁

"You won a prize"

A message says you won money or a gift, but you must pay a "fee" first.

👨‍👩‍👧

"Family emergency"

A message from an unknown number says it is your child and urgently asks for money.

Warning signs of a scam

  • It makes you feel rushed or scared ("Act now or your account will close!").
  • It asks for your password, PIN, OTP code or card number.
  • It comes from an unknown number or a strange website address.
  • It has spelling mistakes or an offer that is too good to be true.

Creating a strong password

  1. Use a short sentence you can remember, for example MyGardenHas3Palms!
  2. Use a different password for your email and your bank.
  3. Never use your birthday, name or 123456.
  4. Turn on two-step verification when an app offers it.

Quiz: Spot the Scam

Read each message and decide: is it safe or a scam?

1. SMS: "Your bank card is blocked. Click bit.ly/xyz to unlock it now."

Tip: Banks never send links to "unlock" cards. Call the number on the back of your card.

2. Your daughter calls from her usual number and asks if you need anything from the shop.

Tip: A known number, a normal request and no money or codes asked for. This is safe.

3. A caller says he is from your bank and asks you to read him the 6-digit code you just received.

Tip: Never share an OTP code. Hang up and call your bank yourself.

What to do if you are scammed

  1. Call your bank immediately using the number on your card, and ask them to block it.
  2. Change your passwords.
  3. Report it to the police and keep the messages as evidence.
  4. Tell your family. It is not your fault, and talking about it protects others.
